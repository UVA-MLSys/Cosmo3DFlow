Abstract

We present Cosmo3DFlow, a generative framework combining 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform with flow matching to reconstruct early-Universe initial conditions from present-day observations. The wavelet transform addresses the void problem — ~63.7% of cosmic volume is near-empty — by converting spatial sparsity into spectral sparsity, enabling stable ODE integration with large step sizes. At 128³ resolution, Cosmo3DFlow achieves 50× faster sampling than score-based diffusion (5.2 s vs. 243 s), with better reconstruction quality and half the peak memory.

The Void Problem

🌌

63.7% Empty by Volume

Voids hold only 16.2% of dark matter mass — yet voxel models spend equal compute on every cell.

🌀

Spectral Sparsity

3D Haar DWT converts spatial emptiness into near-zero high-frequency coefficients, making sparsity explicit.

⚡

8× Compression

One DWT level halves each dimension → 8× fewer voxels → 5× lower per-step cost.

Voxel vs. wavelet representation. Left: uniform compute over 1.3 M empty voxels at 128³. Right: voids collapse to a few coarse coefficients; filaments and halos get fine-grained detail.

Method

1

Transform

3D Haar DWT → 8 coefficient tensors at ½ spatial resolution (8× compression)

2

Interpolate

Linear flow path in wavelet space between noise and target

3

Train

Flow matching loss + power spectrum regularizer (λ = 0.01)

4

Sample

100 Euler steps in wavelet space → IDWT → density field

Wavelet-Aware 3D U-Net

Architecture. 16-ch input (8ch noise + 8ch conditioning) → 8-ch velocity field. Scale-specific conditioning injects per-level wavelet features via 1×1×1 convolutions; cross-scale skips bridge encoder to non-corresponding decoder levels.
